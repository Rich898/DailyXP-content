# VitalYOU — Trustpilot stand-up playbook (1 Sep 2026)

_Companion to `VITALYOU-REVIEWS-EVAL.md` (the platform decision). This is the execution
doc: from zero to 20+ live reviews in 30 days, off the back of the first 100 customers,
before anything points at it from the site._

## Objective and definition of done

- **20+ reviews live within 30 days**, overwhelmingly 4–5★, all from genuine customers.
- **TrustScore ≥ 4.5** — see the maths below for why 5.0 is not the number to expect.
- All ~100 customers invited by end of month 2 (50/mo is the free-plan invitation cap —
  the cap sets the rhythm, and inviting the full base is also what makes the profile
  bulletproof if Trustpilot's fraud team ever looks).
- Site untouched until the flip criteria at the bottom are met.

## The maths (set expectations before the first invite)

**Reviews needed:** with ~100 customers, 20+ is comfortably reachable without stretching:

| Channel | Pool | Conversion (realistic) | Reviews |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tier A — personal call/text from founder | 20–25 warmest | 40–60% | 9–14 |
| Tier B — Trustpilot email invitation | 50 (month-1 cap) | 10–20% (+auto-reminder) | 5–10 |
| Top-up — SMS/WhatsApp with review link | as needed | 15–25% | 3–6 |

Month-1 expected landing: **17–28 reviews.** Tier A is the engine — protect the time for
those personal touches; they convert at 3–4× email.

**Score to expect:** TrustScore seeds every new profile with a prior of ~7 phantom reviews
at 3.5★ that wash out as real reviews land. All-5★ maths:

- 20 reviews → (20×5 + 7×3.5) / 27 ≈ **4.6**
- 30 reviews → ≈ **4.7**
- 50 reviews → ≈ **4.8**

So a 4.6 at 20 reviews **is** the all-fives outcome, not a problem to fix — and the
argument for keeping the machine running toward 50 over the quarter. (Recency and
frequency also weight the score: a steady trickle beats a spike, which conveniently is
also the pattern that never trips review filters.)

**Display mechanics for week 1:** a zero-review claimed profile shows "0 reviews" and no
score (reads *new*, not mediocre — the phantom baseline is internal only), and invitees
via the evaluate link land straight on the review form, not the scored page. The score
appears with review #1 at ~3.7 and climbs through the label bands: ~4 all-5★ reviews
crosses into "Great" (≈4.05), **~8–9 crosses into "Excellent" (≥4.3)**. That 1–8 review
zone is the whole reason Tier A goes first — punch through it in the first two weeks
while the only visitors are our own invitees.

## Days 0–2 — setup checklist

1. **Check for an existing profile first**: `trustpilot.com/review/<vitalyou-domain>` —
   unclaimed profiles can pre-exist with stray reviews. Claim whatever is there.
2. Create the free business account at `business.trustpilot.com` against the VitalYOU
   domain; verify via company email/domain.
3. Complete the profile fully — logo, cover, one-paragraph description, correct
   health category, website link, support email/phone. A bare profile reads abandoned;
   completeness is free conversion.
4. Notifications ON, routed to a nominated owner; target = every review replied to
   within 48h from day one.
5. Capture the two URLs: the public profile page, and the direct review link
   (`trustpilot.com/evaluate/<domain>`) — the evaluate link is the one that goes in
   texts and follow-ups, unlimited and free.
6. Load the customer email list into the invitation tool (name, email, reference) but
   **send nothing yet** — batches are scheduled in the calendar below.

## Days 2–4 — the list

Work the 100 customers into a sheet with four columns: name, contact, tier, invite date.

- **Exclusions first**: staff, family, suppliers, anyone with a stake. They cost more
  than they add — a single connected reviewer is the classic removal trigger.
- **Tier A (20–25)**: the warmest — long-standing, most engaged, best relationship.
  Founder-personal channel.
- **Tier B (everyone else)**: email via Trustpilot's invitation system (their invites
  carry the "Invited" label and an automatic reminder — worth more than a bcc blast).
- Order within tiers doesn't matter; what matters is **everyone ends up invited** —
  warmest first is sequencing, and fine; the invite list matching the customer list is
  what makes the profile audit-proof.

## The ask — scripts and templates

The angle that does the work: **the 100th-customer milestone.** It's true, it's warm, it
gives a reason for the ask that isn't "please rate us."

**Tier A phone/voice-note script:**

> Hey [name] — quick one. We just hit our 100th customer, which you helped get us to.
> I'm setting up our Trustpilot page so new people can see real experiences. Two things —
> first, honestly, how's it all been for you? Anything we should be doing better?
> [listen — this is the feedback round, take notes]
> Would you be up for putting that into a quick review? Two minutes, write whatever
> you'd honestly tell a friend. I'll text you the link.

Then send the link **to everyone you called, same day, via the Trustpilot invitation
tool** ("I'll email you the link now") — not the bare evaluate link. Invitation-tool
reviews carry an attached invite record and the "Invited" label, and a 0→10 fortnight of
invited reviews reads as textbook onboarding to Trustpilot, where ten organic-link
reviews with no trail on a day-old profile reads as a burst. A call list that matches
the invite list is what a clean pattern looks like. (Evaluate link = SMS-only people and
later top-ups.)

**Tier B founder email** (send via Trustpilot invitations where possible; this text works
standalone too):

> **Subject: You were one of our first 100 — two quick things**
>
> Hi [name],
>
> Small milestone this week — VitalYOU just reached 100 customers, and you're one of
> the people who got us there. Two things, both optional:
>
> 1. Hit reply and tell me one thing we could be doing better. Goes straight to me.
> 2. We've just opened our Trustpilot page. If you're willing, a short review of your
>    honest experience — whatever you'd tell a friend — would genuinely help people
>    find us. Takes about two minutes: [link]
>
> Thanks for being part of the first hundred.
> [Founder], VitalYOU
>
> P.S. No perks or discounts attached to reviews — Trustpilot (rightly) bans that.
> Just honestly useful to us.

**SMS/WhatsApp top-up:**

> Hi [name], [Founder] from VitalYOU — we just hit 100 customers and you're one of
> them 🎉 We've opened our Trustpilot page; if you've got 2 minutes, an honest review
> would mean a lot: [evaluate link]. No pressure either way!

**Reminder (day 6–7, non-responders, once):** Trustpilot's system auto-reminds Tier B.
For manual sends: "Hi [name] — no worries if it's not your thing, but if the review was
on the maybe-pile, here's the link again: [link]. Either way, thanks."

## The 30-day calendar

| Week | Action | Expected cumulative |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Setup done. Tier A wave 1: 10–12 personal calls/texts. First replies to reviews same-week. | 4–7 live |
| **2** | Tier A wave 2 (remainder). Tier B batch 1: 25 Trustpilot invitations. | 10–15 |
| **3** | Tier B batch 2: 25 invitations (hits the 50/mo cap). Manual reminders to Tier A maybes. **Checkpoint: if under 12 live, fire the SMS top-up wave.** | 15–22 |
| **4** | Auto-reminders land for Tier B. Sweep stragglers. Prep (don't publish) the site badge. | 20–28 |
| **Month 2** | Remaining ~50 customers invited under the new month's cap. Flip the site when criteria met. | 25–40 |

**Pacing rule that protects the count: compress the asks, not the arrivals.** Tier A
calls happen across days 1–3; arrival lag (people review 0–7 days after an ask) spreads
the landings into a natural day 1–12 drip on its own. Never orchestrate a posting moment
("everyone do it Tuesday" is how ten land in 48h). Working target: **8–10 live by day
10–14, no more than ~2–3 landing on any single day in week 1** — if a day runs hot, hold
the next asks a day. Nobody but our own invitees sees the page during the low-score
zone, so a slow-looking fortnight costs nothing; a burst-triggered removal costs real
reviews and flags a day-old profile. Thereafter: batches of 10–15 invites every few
days, never the whole list in a day. Same logic: reviews come from customers' own
phones/laptops on their own networks — never a "leave it now" moment on clinic wifi or
a shared device at reception.

## Operating rules (all mechanical — each one exists because breaking it deletes reviews)

1. **Same link, everyone.** The feedback question and the review link travel together in
   one message to the whole list. The moment the link only follows positive feedback,
   the invite pattern itself shows it — that's the one pattern Trustpilot's fraud model
   and a public warning banner are built around. Ask-everyone costs a couple of 4★s and
   buys immunity.
2. **Nothing in exchange. Ever.** No discount, entry, or freebie — one reviewer
   mentioning a perk is a removal-and-banner event. (The email P.S. handles this
   preemptively and reads as integrity.)
3. **No scripting content.** "Write what you'd tell a friend" is the whole instruction.
   Customers mentioning clinical stuff in their reviews is fine — on Trustpilot's page
   it's their speech, not our advertising.
4. **Reply to every review within 48h.** Thank-yous varied and short. For anything under
   5★: thank, acknowledge, take it offline ("we'd love to make this right — email
   [support]"). One care point: replies shouldn't confirm or expand clinical details a
   reviewer shares (keep replies to service and gratitude — the AHPRA reply rule from
   the eval doc).
5. **A stray 3★ is not an emergency.** Respond well and keep collecting — 22 reviews at
   4.7 with a thoughtful owner reply outconverts a suspicious wall of tens. Do not chase
   removals unless a review is actually fake, and use the flag flow if it is.

## Flip criteria (when the site starts pointing at it)

All three, then ship: **(a)** 20+ reviews live, **(b)** TrustScore ≥ 4.5, **(c)** replies
up to date. What ships = the score-only badge TrustBox + link to the profile (tier 2 in
`VITALYOU-REVIEWS-EVAL.md` — score and count, no review text on our pages).

## After the sprint — make it a system

The 100-customer backlog is a one-off asset; steady state is the ask baked into the
customer journey at one fixed experience point (e.g. N weeks in, or program completion),
sent to every customer who reaches it via the 50/mo invitation quota. Frequency and
recency feed TrustScore directly, so ~10–15/month on autopilot beats another sprint —
and it builds toward the 100-reviews-in-12-months line that unlocks Google Seller Rating
stars if paid ads ever start.

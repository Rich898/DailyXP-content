# VitalYOU — review platform evaluation: Trustpilot vs Google (31 Aug 2026)

_Decision doc for standing up VitalYOU's first customer reviews. Constraint given: the
first wave comes from friendly customers, and nothing is published on the VitalYOU site
initially. Generic strategy only — no customer or health data in this doc._

## Decision, in three lines

1. **For the quiet first phase: Trustpilot (free plan).** It fits the staged rollout —
   public but low-traffic until you point people at it, invitation-verified reviews, and
   the on-site widget is ready for the moment you *do* want reviews on the site.
2. **For the endgame: Google — if VitalYOU is eligible** (see the gate below). In
   Australia, the rating in brand search and Maps is where trust actually converts.
   They're complementary, not either/or; the real question is sequencing.
3. **If "not public at all initially" is a hard requirement: neither.** Collect direct
   testimonials first (form/email, with written consent to republish later), then convert
   those same customers into platform reviews at go-public. No review platform has a
   private mode.

## THE TRAP — "not published initially" doesn't mean what it sounds like

**Every review on either platform is public on the platform the minute it's posted.**
The only thing under our control is whether we embed them on vitalyou's own site — which
is optional on both, forever. What differs is *discoverability*:

- **Google**: the star rating and count appear in the brand-search knowledge panel and on
  Maps **from review #1**. Anyone googling "VitalYOU" sees it. There is no ramp — going
  live on Google *is* going public, at maximum visibility, with a small-n average (one
  early 3★ among five reviews prints 4.6★ on every brand search).
- **Trustpilot**: reviews live at `trustpilot.com/review/<domain>` — public, but nobody
  sees it unless they go looking. Within weeks it will rank for "VitalYOU reviews"
  searches, but it doesn't sit on the brand search itself. This is the closest thing to a
  quiet start a real platform offers.

**Both are one-way doors.** Google reviews can't be switched off or hidden, ever. A
Trustpilot profile page exists for the domain once anyone reviews it and the business
can't delete it. Start a platform only when comfortable that its page is permanent.

## Decision gate — is VitalYOU even eligible for Google reviews?

Google reviews hang off a **Google Business Profile**, and GBP requires **in-person
contact with customers** — a storefront, or a service area you visit customers in.
**Online-only businesses are not eligible** (Google's representation guidelines say so
explicitly; virtual-office addresses are also banned). Telehealth/virtual-only sits in a
grey zone Google mostly resolves against eligibility.

- VitalYOU sees customers in person (clinic/studio/home visits) → eligible; Google is on
  the table and is the long-run priority.
- VitalYOU is online-only → Google is effectively off the table and **the decision makes
  itself: Trustpilot** (with ProductReview.com.au worth a look as the AU-local
  complement — it's the local incumbent in many consumer categories).

## Head to head

| | **Google** | **Trustpilot** |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Free, entirely | Free plan is real (claim profile, ~50 email invites/mo, reply, flag, 1 basic widget); useful extras are paid, ~US$250–1,000+/mo, **annual commitment** |
| Where reviews show | Brand search + Maps — maximum visibility, immediately, not optional | Trustpilot profile page; on our site only if/when we embed |
| Fits the quiet start | Poorly — public at full blast from review #1 | Well — public but low-traffic until we point at it |
| Reviewer verification | None — any Google account can review, customer or not | Invited reviews are marked verified; open reviews can be challenged with an evidence flow |
| Local/SEO value (AU) | High — local pack, Maps, brand-search conversion | None for local SEO; profile ranks for "[brand] reviews"; feeds **Google Seller Ratings** (ad stars, needs ~100 reviews/12mo, ≥3.5★) |
| Consumer recognition in AU | Universal | Moderate (strongest in UK/EU); ProductReview.com.au is the local incumbent |
| On-site embedding later | No official widget (third parties only); self-serving star markup earns nothing | Core product — TrustBox widgets, though the good ones are paid |
| Removal of unfair reviews | Slow, opaque flagging; sabotage hard to shift | Structured flag + reviewer-verification process |
| Risk profile for our plan | New profile + burst of reviews from low-history accounts/same network gets **silently filtered**; April 2026 Gemini-powered enforcement also bans staff quotas, scripts, name-dropping staff, on-site pressure | **Cherry-picking is a named offence** — selective inviting can earn a public Consumer Alert banner on the profile |
| Eligibility | In-person contact required; verification (often video) can be painful; suspensions happen | Any business with a domain |

## The friendly-customer playbook (what keeps it legal and un-deleted)

The plan — genuine customers we know, asked to share their honest experience — is fine on
both platforms **if run like this**:

1. **Genuine customers, own words, own devices, own time.** Never from our office wifi,
   never at an event, never scripted. (Google's filter kills same-network/low-history
   bursts silently; scripts and staff-name coaching are explicitly banned since Apr 2026.)
2. **No incentives. None.** No discount, gift, or freebie for a review. Both platforms ban
   it, and the ACCC treats undisclosed incentivised reviews as misleading conduct under
   the ACL — this is an active enforcement priority, and penalties are corporate-scale.
3. **Employees, family, and anyone with a stake don't review.** Both platforms + ACL.
4. **Ask, don't gate.** A one-off "we're standing up our profile, would you share your
   experience" to customers is normal. A *system* that asks happy customers and routes
   unhappy ones elsewhere is review gating — banned by Google, banned as cherry-picking by
   Trustpilot (their rule: invite consistently and fairly, everyone the same way).
5. **Drip, don't burst.** A few reviews over weeks reads organic; fifteen in a weekend on
   a new profile reads fake to both platforms' pattern detection — likeliest outcome is
   the reviews quietly vanish, worst case a public warning banner (Trustpilot) or profile
   restriction (Google).
6. **On Trustpilot, use the invitation flow** (free plan covers ~50/mo — far more than
   needed) rather than pasting a link around: invited reviews carry the "verified" mark
   and are far more defensible.

## If VitalYOU is a regulated health service (AHPRA)

Only relevant if AHPRA-registered practitioners deliver the service — but if so it's
load-bearing:

- **Reviews sitting on Google/Trustpilot are fine** — AHPRA treats platforms you can't
  switch off as outside your control, and patients are free to post.
- **Using them in our own advertising is not.** Embedding reviews that speak to *clinical*
  aspects (outcomes, treatment success) on the VitalYOU site or socials is a testimonial
  under the National Law s133 — prohibited. Non-clinical comments (service, communication,
  facilities) are usable. This makes the "nothing on our site initially" instinct not just
  cautious but compliant — and it means the later embed step needs a clinical/non-clinical
  filter, which favours a platform whose widgets can show curated/tagged subsets
  (Trustpilot, paid) or simply hand-picked non-clinical quotes.
- Replying to reviews that recount clinical detail needs care too.

If VitalYOU is wellness/non-regulated, ignore this section; ACL rules above still apply
(and TGA advertising rules if therapeutic goods are involved — testimonials there carry
their own disclosure/verification requirements).

## Recommended sequence

1. **Now (quiet phase):** collect direct testimonials with consent (owned, genuinely
   private) **and/or** claim the Trustpilot free profile and invite the friendly-customer
   round through its invitation flow, dripped over 4–6 weeks. Don't embed anything.
2. **Go-public:** if eligible, stand up the Google Business Profile *when ready to be
   seen*, and from then on ask every customer, alternating/offering both platforms.
   Google becomes the primary ask in AU; Trustpilot keeps collecting via invitations.
3. **Site embed (later):** Trustpilot TrustBox (free one first; pay only if the widget set
   earns it — note the annual commitment) — filtered for non-clinical content if AHPRA
   applies.
4. **Only if ad spend arrives:** Trustpilot volume (100+/12mo) unlocks Google Seller
   Rating stars in ads.

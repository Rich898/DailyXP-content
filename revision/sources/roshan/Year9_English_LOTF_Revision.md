## 1. The key idea: Freud's psychoanalytical theory

| Part of the mind | What it is | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Id** | Immediate gratification of wants and desires; primal instinct | **Jack** |
| **Superego** | Moral standards; fear of punishment controls behaviour | **Piggy** |
| **Ego** | Tries to satisfy the id's desires in appropriate ways; reason and logic | **Ralph** |

- The id is kept in check by **social conditioning**: school, family, police and government.
- On the island those institutions are gone, so the boys slide into savagery.

**The conch**
- A symbol of power and authority, and of order amid chaos.
- Whoever holds it has the right to speak.
- It links to the social conditioning the boys learnt from school and adults, so it stands for the **superego**.
- Watch for moments when the boys do and don't listen to it. Those moments show a change in mindset.

**Example of the boys resisting the id:** Roger throws stones at Henry but deliberately misses, because he still fears punishment. The superego is controlling him even though he feels the pull of the id.

## 2. Characters (school revision sheet)

### Jack – gives in to the id
- **What happens to him:** he devolves and regresses. His behaviour is driven by primal instincts and he becomes animalistic.
- **What he values:** tracking and hunting the pig. He forgets about getting rescued.
- **How he acts:** he wants dominance and leadership, becomes violent and wants to inflict harm. He resists the rules Ralph tries to keep.
- **Key quotes:**
  - "ape-like", "crouching", "hissing" – **zoomorphism** (savage behaviour)
  - "Kill the pig, cut her throat, spill her blood" – **repeated chant** (mob mentality lets the id take control)

### Ralph – rational and logical
- **What he values:** getting off the island (rescue), keeping civilised behaviour, rules, jobs, keeping the fire going. This contrasts with Jack.
- **How he thinks:** he sees the temptation of the id but isn't controlled by it. He sees the big picture.
- **The weight he carries:** he feels burdened by the need to keep social structures going (the conch, order, rules).
- **Key quotes:**
  - "If I blow the conch and they don't come back then we've had it" – realisation or epiphany
  - "We'll have rules" – declaration
  - "You let the fire out" – repetition
  - "We'll be like animals" – simile

### Comparing Jack and Ralph – the "where / whereas" rule
> **Quote:** "They walked along, two continents of experience and feeling, unable to communicate."
> **Example topic sentence:** In this extract, Ralph and Jack's attempts to maintain a civilised relationship are contrasted, **where** Ralph wants rules which represents the order of the superego, **whereas** Jack's obsession with hunting represents the chaos of the id.

## 3. THE MODEL ESSAY (school's 2026 version – learn this)

### Full version

**INTRODUCTION**
- **Thesis (Yeah):** Human behaviour is shaped by the reasoning of the ego.
- **Thesis (Nah):** However, when the id is not balanced by the superego, humanity becomes savage and chaotic.
- **Trident:** William Golding's novel, *Lord of the Flies* (1954), explores the conflict between the id and the superego, through the moral struggles of a group of British school boys stranded on a deserted island, revealing that without rules and order, society descends into chaos.

**BODY ONE**
- **Topic:** Golding suggests that morality is required to maintain a stable society.
- **Idea 1:** Initially, the boys' behaviour is influenced by what they have learnt from their parents and society.
- **Decon 1:** The personification, as the boys "obeyed the summons of the conch", highlights how their superego attempts to recreate the behaviours from home on the island.
- **Idea 2:** Despite their freedom, the boys still fear consequences for their actions.
- **Decon 2:** Choose one of these:
  - Whilst throwing rocks at Henry and deliberately missing, Roger symbolically recognises that he is surrounded by the "protection of parents and school and policemen", highlighting that his morality is ruled by the fear of punishment his parents would give him.
  - Despite the lack of adults, Maurice feels guilty there was no parent to symbolically "let the heavy hand fall", highlighting that his morality is ruled by the fear of punishment his parents would give him.
- **Idea 3:** However, without enforced punishments to maintain the boys' superegos, their behaviour becomes cruel.
- **Decon 3:** Jack's unkind response to Piggy, exclaiming "you shut up!" / "shut up, Fatty!", highlights the growing influence of the id due to an absence of punishment.
- **Wrap:** Thus, Golding hints that without consequences to uphold good behaviour, a stable society will fall apart as the id controls their choices.

**BODY TWO**
- **Topic:** Golding examines the loss of our collective humanity and morality in a society without rules.
- **Idea 1:** Jack's rebellion against the rules of the boys' society signals their loss of morality and the rise of the savage id.
- **Decon 1:** His rude remark, exclaiming "Bollocks to the rules! We're strong – we hunt!", demonstrates the growing loss of connection to society and civilisation.
- **Idea 2:** Following this, Jack submits to the wildness and savagery of the id.
- **Decon 2:** The use of simile, describing Jack as "ape-like among the tangle of trees", highlights his loss of morality, demonstrating his embrace of the primitive, animalistic id.
- **Idea 3:** Golding's message finds voice in the boys' collective descent into savagery, signalling their loss of rationality and morality.
- **Decon 3:** The use of repetition, as the boys chant "Kill the beast! Cut his throat! Spill his blood!", highlights their regression into violence and wildness, resulting from their separation from society.
- **Wrap:** In this way, Golding reveals the need for the rules and order of society to maintain morality and resist the temptation of the id.

**CONCLUSION**
- **Broad statement:** Freudian theory suggests that the reasoning of the ego is taught by the integrity of the superego, tempering the id.
- **Bident:** Golding's novel challenges this notion, through the devolution of a group of British school children stranded on a deserted island, highlighting that the freedoms offered by isolation from the order of society will result in chaos and savagery.

### Super-short version (292 words – for when time is tight)
- **Intro:** same as above.
- **Single body paragraph:**
  - Topic: morality is required to maintain a stable society.
  - Decon 1: the boys "obeyed the summons of the conch" (superego).
  - Idea shift: without rules and consequences, the boys lose their morality and give in to the temptation of the id.
  - Decon 2: Jack's "Bollocks to the rules! We're strong – we hunt!"
  - Decon 3: the chant "Kill the beast! Cut his throat! Spill his blood!"
  - Wrap: same as Body One's wrap.
- **Conclusion:** broad statement as above. Bident: "Golding's novel challenges this notion through the school children and their descent into chaos and savagery when isolated on the island."

## 4. Writing tools (school revision sheet)

### Topic sentences must include
- The words of the question
- The key verb from the question
- An overarching idea, concept or emotion
- A brief overview of what's happening in the extract

> **Example:**
> Q: How does the extract symbolise a change in Jack?
> A: The author symbolises a change in Jack as we witness a shift of his behaviour from civil to animalistic, signalling how he is becoming a manifestation of the id.

### Decon formula: Technique → Example → Connect → Effect
- **Technique:** "Golding's use of [technique] describing how…"
- **Example:** "QUOTE"
- **Connect:** conveys / highlights / gives voice to / reinforces / positioning us / accentuating
- **Effect:** don't explain the literal meaning. Link back to the idea in the topic sentence.
- **Tip for more than one effect:** go from *captures* → *highlighting / reinforcing* → a final big effect (the author's purpose) with *gives voice to / positioning us / accentuating*.

> **Model decon:** The littluns obey the personified "summons of the conch", capturing the shell's enormous symbolic influence in transplanting previously acquired social instructions within their new, foreign environment, accentuating that the boys are still conditioned by their civil institutions.

**Segues:** Furthermore, In addition, Therefore, Moreover, Ultimately.

### Effect phrase bank
**Savagery (id)**
- …conveys his gradual transformation/devolution from civilised British choir boy to savage hunter
- …suggesting his behaviour is being regulated by the primal id
- …reinforcing that his ability to reason and use his ego is giving way to primal instincts governed by the id
- …portraying his moral descent into the savage, primitive desires of the id
- …liberates him from his hindering humane conditioning, enabling him to fully embrace the id and descend into shameless animalistic savagery
- …highlights its dehumanising effect on Jack, exacerbating the process of social devolution
- …broadening the divide / the collapse of unity within the group as they succumb to the savage desires of the id

**Civilised (superego)**
- …accentuating the boys' enthusiastic attempts to imitate a democratic society reminiscent of their familiar British social order
- …acknowledging that they are recreating a structure influenced by the superego
- …suggests the shell holds the surprising power to moderate the boys' behaviour despite their unsupervised isolation
- …behaviour remains governed by the consequences implemented by his parents and teachers
- …consequences fortified by his social conditioning, reaffirming the conflict between his primal instincts and civility

### Short-response structures
- **3 marks:** topic sentence → decon → segue → decon
- **4 marks:** topic sentence → decon → segue → decon → segue → decon
- **5 marks:** topic sentence → decon 1 → **idea shift sentence** → decon 2 → segue → decon 3

School's 5-mark templates:
1. **Civil vs savage:**
   - Decon 1 civil (superego)
   - Idea shift: the superego dissolves and the id rises
   - Decon 2 savage (id)
   - Furthermore, decon 3 savage
2. **Paradise / utopia:**
   - Decon 1 paradise
   - Decon 2 paradise and social harmony
   - Idea shift: paradise disrupted by savagery
   - Furthermore, decon 3 savagery
3. **Law and order vs primal urges:**
   - Decon 1 rules and social institutions
   - Idea shift: social order breaks and devolution begins
   - Decon 2 collapse of society
   - Furthermore, decon 3 chaos unfolds

## 5. Chapter summaries
**Chapters 1–9 (school revision sheet)**
1. **The Sound of the Shell** – the boys crash on the island. Ralph and Piggy find the conch and use it to gather the group.
2. **Fire on the Mountain** – rules and leadership are discussed. A signal fire spreads out of control, killing a littlun.
3. **Huts on the Beach** – Ralph and Simon build shelters while Jack becomes obsessed with hunting, and tension grows.
4. **Painted Faces and Long Hair** – Jack paints his face and embraces savagery. The signal fire goes out and they miss a rescue.
5. **Beast from Water** – Ralph calls a meeting. Fear of the "beast" spreads and unity starts to crumble.
6. **Beast from Air** – a dead parachutist lands. Sam and Eric mistake it for the beast, and fear intensifies.
7. **Shadows and Tall Trees** – the boys hunt and grow wilder. Ralph joins in and feels the thrill of violence.
8. **Gift for the Darkness** – Jack splits off and forms his own tribe. The pig's head ("Lord of the Flies") is offered to the beast.
9. **A View to a Death** – Simon discovers the beast is the parachutist. When he tries to tell the boys, he is killed in a frenzied dance.

**Chapters 10–12 (Claude – not on the revision sheet)**

10. **The Shell and the Glasses** – Jack's tribe raids Ralph's camp at night and steals Piggy's glasses, so they control fire.
11. **Castle Rock** – Ralph and Piggy go to demand the glasses back. Roger rolls a boulder that kills Piggy and **shatters the conch**: the end of order and of the superego.
12. **Cry of the Hunters** – Jack's tribe hunts Ralph and sets the island on fire. A naval officer arrives and rescues them, and Ralph weeps for "the end of innocence, the darkness of man's heart".

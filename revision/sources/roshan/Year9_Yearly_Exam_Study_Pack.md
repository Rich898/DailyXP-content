## Part A – Chemistry of New Substances

### Model answers from the teacher's notification (learn these structures)

**1. Explain the changes in mass in system A (open) and B (closed) as the reaction proceeds, referring to conservation of mass.**
> The total mass of reactants is equal to the total mass of products in both A and B. However, carbon dioxide gas is produced in the reaction, escaping the open system in A, resulting in a mass decrease. Alternatively, the gas is unable to escape the closed system in B, resulting in a constant mass.

**2. Justify whether elephant's toothpaste is a decomposition or synthesis reaction.** *(The exam may ask this about a DIFFERENT reaction.)*
> Decomposition reactions involve one reactant breaking down into two or more products. Elephant's toothpaste involves one reactant, hydrogen peroxide, breaking down into two products, oxygen and water, allowing it to be classified as a decomposition reaction.

**3. Compare covalent and ionic bonding, providing an example of each.**
> Both covalent and ionic bonding achieve atomic stability by filling their outer electron shells. However, covalent bonding occurs between non-metals, sharing electrons and forming individual molecules, e.g. H₂O, CO₂, O₂. Whereas ionic bonding occurs between a metal and a non-metal, forming extensive lattices of charged ions, e.g. NaCl, CaCO₃.

## Part B – Global Patterns

### Model answers from the notification

**1. Discuss Alfred Wegener's contribution to the theory of continental drift.**
> The Theory of Continental Drift states that the continents were once joined but have drifted apart over time. Alfred Wegener observed that the continents fit together similar to a jigsaw puzzle, causing him to collect evidence that there were similar rock types, mountain formations and fossil evidence across different continents that are now separated by oceans. He hypothesised that they could have once been joined in a supercontinent which he called Pangaea, suggesting that the continents drifted apart over time, resulting in the many continents we see today. However, Wegener was not a geologist and failed to suggest a reason to explain how the continents could move, causing other scientists to dismiss his theory at the time.

**2. Construct annotated diagrams of the 3 fault types (normal, reverse, strike-slip).**
- Normal fault: the crust is pulled apart (tension) and the hanging wall slides **down**.
- Reverse fault: the crust is pushed together (compression) and the hanging wall slides **up**.
- Strike-slip fault: the blocks slide **sideways** past each other.

**3. Distinguish between convergent and divergent boundaries.**
> A convergent plate boundary is where two tectonic plates move towards each other, causing continental crust to compress or oceanic crust to subduct, forming geological features such as mountain ranges, trenches or volcanic islands. Whereas, a divergent boundary is where two tectonic plates move away from each other, causing continental crust to rift or magma to rise from the mantle, creating new oceanic crust, resulting in the formation of mid-oceanic ridges.

## Model answers from Canvas (Week 6.3 Exam Revision)

**Fiscal policy**
> Fiscal policy in Australia, controlled by the government, uses government spending and taxation to shape economic outcomes. Employing expansionary fiscal policy during downturns involves heightened spending on infrastructure (creating jobs) and tax cuts to stimulate activity (increasing disposable incomes), injecting funds into the circular flow as people have more money to spend. For example, with less tax to pay a consumer might spend their money on something else, ultimately creating jobs. Conversely, contractionary fiscal policy aims to curb inflation by cutting government spending and raising taxes, acting as withdrawals from the circular flow. In the circular flow of income, leakages, such as savings, taxes, and imports, represent exits, while injections, including investment, government spending, and exports, denote entries. Balancing these factors is crucial for sustaining a stable and growing economy, requiring nuanced policy adjustments.

**Monetary policy**
> Monetary policy in Australia, controlled by the Reserve Bank, manipulates the cash rate (interest rates) to influence the economy. In expansionary phases, interest rates drop, promoting borrowing and spending, injecting funds into the circular flow. Contrarily, during contractions, rates rise, curbing spending to prevent inflation, acting as withdrawals. In the circular flow, leakages like savings, taxes, and imports impact withdrawals, while injections, including investments and exports, contribute to the flow. For example, an expansionary policy could boost home loans, fostering construction, while a contractionary move might restrict borrowing, moderating inflationary pressures but potentially slowing economic growth. For mortgage holders, a reduction in the cost of mortgage repayments means a higher disposable income, allowing money to be spent on other things. For example, a new kitchen, creating employment. Balancing these factors is pivotal for economic stability.

## Writing structured responses – the school's format (from the Sample Answers sheet)
The school teaches a **Topic → linking word → knowledge** scaffold. The three sample answers below were for half-yearly topics, but use the same structure for the yearly topics.

**Cross contamination – "Identify and explain how cross contamination can occur in the commercial kitchen."**
- **Is…** when bacteria is transferred from one object to another, or from raw food to cooked food.
- **Occurs…** when a chef uses the same knife or the same chopping board for raw and cooked ingredients, e.g. raw and cooked chicken on the yellow board.
- **Causes…** food poisoning. Symptoms include vomiting, diarrhoea, headaches, tummy cramps and even death.
- **Avoided…** by using the yellow board for raw chicken and the brown board for cooked chicken, and by washing the knife after cutting raw chicken.

**The danger zone – "Explain why it is important to keep food out of the danger zone."**
- **Is…** the temperature range between **5 °C and 60 °C**, where dangerous bacteria grow and multiply.
- **Affects…** perishable foods such as raw meat and dairy left in this range.
- **Causes…** food to become unsafe and can lead to food poisoning.
- **Can be avoided…** by storing perishable food in the fridge at **4 °C or below**.
- **Example:** a faulty fridge, or ham or raw chicken left on the bench at room temperature for too long.

**Poor workflow – "Discuss how poor workflow can impact a chef in a commercial kitchen."**
- **Is…** being unorganised in the kitchen.
- **Effects…** the group's efficiency in the 50-minute lesson.
- **Caused by…** not following the recipe in logical order, and poor time management.
- **Impacts…** food quality and the time taken to cook and clean up.
- **Improved by…** reading the recipe first, doing tasks in logical order, and communicating clearly.
- **Example:** mise en place not done (e.g. onion not diced for spaghetti bolognaise), or forgetting to preheat the oven.

## ✅ Practice answers for the 3 yearly structured-response topics (Claude's drafts in the school's format)

**Handwashing**
- **Is…** the correct process of washing hands to remove dirt and bacteria before and during food handling.
- **Steps…** wet hands with warm running water, apply soap, scrub palms, backs, between fingers and under nails for about 20 seconds, rinse, and dry with a paper towel.
- **When…** before handling food, after touching raw meat, after the toilet, touching hair or face, sneezing, or handling rubbish.
- **Prevents…** bacteria spreading from hands to food (cross contamination), which can cause food poisoning (vomiting, diarrhoea, cramps).
- **Example:** a chef who handles raw chicken and then makes a salad without washing their hands could make customers sick.

**Cleaning and sanitising**
- **Cleaning is…** removing visible dirt, grease and food scraps using hot water and detergent.
- **Sanitising is…** reducing bacteria to a safe level using a sanitiser or very hot water. It happens **after** cleaning.
- **Order…** scrape, wash with detergent, rinse, sanitise, air dry.
- **Prevents…** bacteria building up on benches, boards and equipment, reducing cross contamination and food poisoning.
- **Example:** chopping boards must be cleaned and sanitised between raw meat and ready-to-eat foods.

**FIFO stock rotation (First In, First Out)**
- **Is…** a storage method where the oldest stock is used first.
- **How…** new deliveries go behind or under older stock, and everything is labelled with dates (received, use-by or best-before).
- **Prevents…** food passing its use-by date, spoiling and being served; reduces food waste and saves the business money.
- **Example:** when a new delivery of milk arrives, it goes at the back of the cool room so the older milk is used first.
